# Ages of Computing Technology

## Stage 1: Transistorization (1945-1962)
Progression from first vacuum-tube powered general-purpose computers to transistorized devices.
Technological advancements occurred through increasing reliability and miniaturization of transistors.
Planar manufacturing techniques eventually led to MOSFET and then CMOS designs that became standard.
CMOS essentially remains the standard, but it has evolved geometry into what is called a FinFET.

## Stage 2: Transistor packaging (1964-1977)
CMOS enabled "printing" of large-scale circuitry on a single silicon wafer, so the next technological advancements came from designing increasingly sophisticated layouts of transistors to perform specific computational tasks, and linking these modules together in progressively larger circuits.
This culminates in the advent of the personal desktop computer market in the late 1970s.

## Stage 3: Frequency maximization (1975-2000)
Once the basic layout of a CPU was established, performance increases came from ratcheting up clock speeds so that the same hardware could do more operations in the same amount of time.
Since tightly-packed transistors have shorter interconnecting wires, the power consumption of a given circuit is determined by size and frequency rather than transistor count (up to a point).
This means the power *density* of a circuit is determined by clock frequency, and since power consumption scales roughly *cubically* with frequency there is a point where normal dissipative cooling systems are insufficient to avoid damage from overheating.
That point was encountered in about 2004, when Intel's newest Pentium chips couldn't push beyond ~4GHz with normal cooling.

Alongside these developments, Moore's Law (established in the prior era) continued apace which meant hardware designers had ever-increasing silicon real estate to work with, and filled it with instruction decoders
